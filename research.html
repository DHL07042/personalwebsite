Research

01

Foot–ground distance sensing

Small time-of-flight (ToF) sensors on the foot measure the distance to the ground throughout the gait cycle. Unlike pressure sensors, they keep reporting during swing, so a robot can see contact coming instead of only registering it.

J1 · JKSPE 2026T1 · ISPS 2021M1 · in preparation

Real-time gait-phase detection

What I did

Designed an overshoe-mounted module with ToF sensors at the outer heel and the fifth metatarsal head, and a threshold-based classifier that identifies gait phases in real time. Sole author of the journal article [J1]; an earlier version was presented at ASME ISPS 2021 [T1].

Evaluation

Compared with pressure-based reference measurements in one participant walking indoors. The distance signal stays informative through swing, where pressure sensors read zero.

Preparing an exoskeleton for ground contact

What I did

Designed and built a foot–ground distance module (custom PCB, ToF sensors), integrated it into the Angel Legs M20 exoskeleton, and ran walking experiments over tandem force plates. This was my M.S. thesis, Terminal Decision Maker Against Upcoming Surface for the Safe Locomotion of Wearable Robots.

Status

A manuscript, FDL: Foot–Ground Distance Layer for Initial-Contact Load Modulation in a Lower-Limb Exoskeleton, is in preparation [M1].

02

Exoskeleton instrumentation and shock-absorption experiments

Measuring foot–ground and hand–crutch forces during exoskeleton walking, to evaluate a shock-absorption mechanism on the shank.

J2 · IJCAS 2023C1 · ICRA 2021J5 · ICROS 2020

What I did

Developed an FSR-based crutch-force module [J5] and pneumatic plantar-pressure modules, built and instrumented a modified WalkON Suit 4, and ran the shock-absorption experiments [C1, J2].

Reported result

In the journal study, the shock absorber with the designed gait pattern reduced the mean peak heel ground-reaction force by 23% compared with no absorber (one participant) [J2]. The journal article extends the ICRA 2021 study [C1].

03

WalkON Suit and CYBATHLON 2020

Hardware work on the team’s powered exoskeletons for the CYBATHLON 2020 Powered Exoskeleton Race.

J3 · IEEE IEM 2022

What I did

Assembled and debugged the team’s exoskeletons through the competition schedule, modified the foot module to fit a wider range of users, and integrated the pressure-sensing foot hardware used for human-intention detection.

Team outcome

Gold and bronze medals in the Powered Exoskeleton Race. Three graduate students on the team, including me, received the KAIST Global Leadership Award. The overview article [J3] describes the WalkON Suit system.

04

Experimental systems for wearable-robot evaluation

An integrated measurement and analysis setup used across the projects above.

Instrumentation

Integrated VICON motion capture, an AMTI instrumented treadmill, Delsys EMG, Xsens inertial sensors, and custom sensors into one evaluation setup.

Analysis and modeling

Wrote the MATLAB analysis software and built human–robot models in OpenSim.
