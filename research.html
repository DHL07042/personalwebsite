01

Foot⁠–⁠ground distance sensing

Time‑of‑flight (ToF) sensors measure foot⁠–⁠ground distance during walking. The distance signal remains available during swing, complementing pressure‑based measurements during stance.

J1 · JKSPE 2026T1 · ISPS 2021M1 · in preparation

Real‑time gait‑phase detection

What I did

Designed a shoe‑mounted module with two ToF sensors, near the lateral heel and the fifth metatarsophalangeal (MTP) joint, and a threshold‑based algorithm that segments the gait cycle into six phases in real time. This work was presented at ASME ISPS 2021 [T1] and subsequently reported in JKSPE [J1].

Evaluation

One healthy participant walked on a flat indoor floor, with about 20 full gait cycles per trial set [J1]. Compared with air‑bladder pressure measurements, the distance‑based method reported swing fractions 3.6 and 5.9 percentage points lower for the left and right foot, respectively (Table 2). It also provided distance measurements during swing [J1].

Design choices. The algorithm defines six gait states from heel and forefoot contact flags, the relative sensor distances, and whether the heel distance is increasing or decreasing. After subtracting the standing offset from each distance signal, small thresholds were used for contact-state detection rather than precise near-ground ranging. During swing, successive heel readings were compared without explicitly estimating velocity [J1, Sections 3.2 and 4.2].

Preparing an exoskeleton for ground contact

What I did

Designed the circuit and wrote the software for a foot⁠–⁠ground distance module (four ToF sensors, STM32 microcontroller). A labmate laid out the PCB from that circuit; I assembled and tested the boards and integrated the module into the Angel Legs M20 exoskeleton. On top of it I implemented a layer that uses the distance signal to retune the joint’s virtual spring and damper before the foot lands, and ran walking experiments over tandem force plates with the layer off and at two settings. This was my M.S. thesis, Terminal Decision Maker Against Upcoming Surface for the Safe Locomotion of Wearable Robots.

Status

A manuscript, FDL: Foot⁠–⁠Ground Distance Layer for Initial‑Contact Load Modulation in a Lower‑Limb Exoskeleton, is in preparation [M1]. It builds on the thesis experiments.

02

Exoskeleton instrumentation and shock‑absorption experiments

Measuring foot⁠–⁠ground and hand–crutch forces during exoskeleton walking, to evaluate a shock‑absorption mechanism on the shank.

J2 · IJCAS 2023C1 · ICRA 2021J5 · ICROS 2020

What I did

Developed an FSR-based crutch‑force module, including the choice of sensor placement [J5], and pneumatic plantar‑pressure modules; fabricated components and instrumented a modified WalkON Suit 4, and ran the shock‑absorption experiments [C1, J2].

Reported result

In the journal study, the shock absorber with the designed gait pattern reduced the mean peak heel ground‑reaction force by 23% compared with no absorber, in one participant [J2]. Forces measured on one crutch were also lower (root‑mean‑square: 11% vertical, 26% horizontal), so the lower heel load was not explained by heavier crutch use [J2]. The journal article extends the ICRA 2021 study [C1].
