Academic research

01 / Non-contact sensing

Gait-phase detection from foot–ground distance

Measure foot–ground distance during stance and swing, and use it to classify gait phases.

My contribution

I developed a time-of-flight (ToF) sensing system and a threshold-based algorithm for gait-phase classification. The journal system uses distance sensors near the heel and forefoot in an overshoe-type module.

Evaluation and scope

The preliminary journal study compared gait-phase classification with pressure-based measurements in one healthy participant during indoor walking. It assessed a sensing method, not clinical effectiveness or control performance across different terrains.

02 / Contact-force measurement

Instrumentation for exoskeleton shock-absorption experiments

Measure foot–ground and hand–crutch interaction forces during exoskeleton walking to evaluate a shank shock-absorption mechanism.

My contribution

I developed a force-sensing resistor (FSR) crutch module and pneumatic plantar-pressure sensing modules. I fabricated and instrumented modified WalkON Suit IV hardware and conducted experiments.

Reported result

The journal study reported a 23% reduction in mean peak heel ground-reaction force with the shock absorber and the designed gait pattern, compared with no absorber, in a single-participant experiment.

This is a research-team result. My contribution was in instrumentation, hardware implementation, and experiments. The journal article extends the 2021 conference study.

03 / Sensing and integration

Foot–ground distance sensing for initial-contact load modulation

Integrate foot–ground distance measurements into a lower-limb exoskeleton for initial-contact load-modulation research.

My contribution

I designed, built, and integrated a foot–ground distance module, including a custom PCB and ToF sensors, into Angel Legs M20 for initial-contact load-modulation research.

Current status

A follow-up manuscript, FDL: Foot–Ground Distance Layer for Initial-Contact Load Modulation in a Lower-Limb Exoskeleton, is in preparation.

04 / Project leadership · 2020

A student-led sensing and data-collection project

KAIST Venture Research Program for Graduate Students · six months · KRW 20 million

My contribution

I formed the team, wrote the proposal, secured funding, and led a project on user- and task-aware exoskeleton-dimension control.

I adapted force-sensing hardware I had developed to build the data-collection system.

Working across groups

My work focused on the robot and data collection. A collaborating research group performed the machine learning using the collected data.

05 / Experimental systems

Instrumentation and analysis for wearable-robot evaluation

Instrumentation

I integrated VICON, an AMTI treadmill, Delsys electromyography (EMG), Xsens, and custom sensors for wearable-robot evaluation.

Analysis and modeling

I developed MATLAB analysis software and human–robot models in OpenSim. This work involved measurement-system integration and experimental data analysis.

06 / Robot integration

WalkON Suit and CYBATHLON 2020

Hardware assembly, debugging, and pressure-sensor integration for the team’s powered exoskeletons.

My contribution

I assembled and debugged the team’s exoskeletons and integrated pressure-sensing foot hardware used for human-intention detection.

Team outcome

The team won gold and bronze medals in the CYBATHLON 2020 Powered Exoskeleton Race. The accompanying overview article describes the broader WalkON Suit system.
